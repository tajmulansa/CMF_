# Vox explainer graphics and motion design: pipeline, tools, formats (research notes, compiled 2026-11-12)

STATUS: first draft, being extended. Source dates are noted inline. Many Vox-staff sources are 2016-2022 and the team has changed a lot since (Johnny Harris, Joss Fong, Estelle Caswell, Phil Edwards and others have left Vox). Anything marked INFERENCE is my reading of the evidence, not something Vox staff stated.

## 1. Team and workflow: who makes the graphics, and how a graphic goes from script to final

### Takeaway
Vox video has historically run on a "one producer owns the video" model, where the producer writes with visuals in mind and often animates in After Effects themselves, supported by an art director and (later) a graphics editor and freelancers. Visual evidence and map/archival material are gathered before or while writing; animation is a late, fast step.

### Cited Findings
- In 2016 Vox's video team had grown from one person (Joe Posner) to 11 full-timers; the executive producer said the team works like independent YouTubers, with one person typically owning a video from pitch to publish, and members shaping the design and look of their own videos. The opening rule was no seated presenters at desks. — [Nieman Lab, Apr 22 2016](https://www.niemanlab.org/?p=124626)
- Estelle Caswell (senior producer, Earworm) says After Effects is her main tool, she taught herself from YouTube tutorials, and she still animates most of her own work. She counted beats and syllables by hand to sync animation to audio, since AE is not built for editing to audio. — [School of Motion podcast (undated; refers to Netflix Explained, so probably 2018-2020, INFERENCE)](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/podcast/estelle-caswell-vox-interview)
- Same interview: she writes with visuals in mind (testing each sentence for what will be on screen), does most effort in research and writing, and treats animation as a comparatively fast late step. She collects archival magazines, footage and photos early. Pitches read like newsroom pitches (headline, angle, visual evidence, experts), not animation briefs. A story editor reviews drafts. — [School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/podcast/estelle-caswell-vox-interview)
- Same interview: team of roughly 25-30 full-time video staff at the time; Dion Lee named as art director; hiring prioritises storytelling ability over animation skill, and many people learned animation on the job. — [School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/podcast/estelle-caswell-vox-interview)
- Joss Fong (senior video producer) described the black hole video: Premiere Pro for the main edit, music and audio mix; After Effects for motion graphics; Photoshop and Illustrator only occasionally; every video has a script editor to flag confusing parts; voice-over needs more explicit signposting than print (pointing viewers at what to look at) and pauses for big ideas. — [The Open Notebook, Jan 7 2020](https://www.theopennotebook.com/2020/01/07/videogram-how-a-vox-video-explains-the-science-behind-the-first-photo-of-a-black-hole/)
- Same article: she delayed the photo reveal so viewers first grasp how hard the observation was; she used Google Earth Studio for telescope sites with moderate camera moves "to add energy". — [The Open Notebook](https://www.theopennotebook.com/2020/01/07/videogram-how-a-vox-video-explains-the-science-behind-the-first-photo-of-a-black-hole/)
- Sam Ellis (senior producer and graphics editor, Vox Atlas) says he works "visuals first but surface-level" (spotting a map that could support a story), then "story first". The Egypt Atlas video took five weeks; episodes run about 10 minutes. — [Storybench, Nov 1 2022](https://www.storybench.org/vox-atlas-producer-sam-ellis-on-his-map-animations/)
- Ed Vega (Vox video producer/editor) on "Kamala Harris, explained in 7 moments": produced in about one week versus the usual three weeks because of the news cycle; the opening screens were a team effort in After Effects; art director asked for a CRT-style jittery filter as a stylistic choice. — [Storybench, Nov 4 2024 (modified Jun 2026)](https://www.storybench.org/?p=22821)
- Former Vox producer Phil Edwards (now independent, so this is post-Vox practice, not Vox process): gathers videos, audio, documents and photos before writing and builds the script around them; about five days to produce after script lock; builds reusable on-screen text and animation templates. — [The Explainers newsletter, Feb 29 2024](https://theexplainers.substack.com/p/making-the-trivialnontrivial-how)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: the planning order that emerges across sources is research and asset gathering, then script written around available visuals, then edit in Premiere with placeholder graphics, then motion graphics in After Effects, then audio. The sources describe pieces of this but no single source lays out the full sequence.
- INFERENCE: because producers animate their own work and art direction is a thin layer (an art director plus templates), Vox graphics tend to be simple, repeatable constructions (masks, parenting, shape layers) rather than bespoke 3D.

### Gaps
- No public document found that shows a Vox video storyboard, style-frame, or animatic stage. The Senior Motion Designer posting mentions style frames and storyboards, but for the Brand Design team, not explainers.
- No source found with per-graphic time budgets (hours per map or chart).

## 2. Software and plugins Vox people say they use

### Takeaway
After Effects is the constant. Premiere Pro for editing, Photoshop and Illustrator for occasional assets, Google Earth Studio for 3D terrain/satellite camera moves, and (for Johnny Harris-era Borders/maps) the GEOlayers plugin. 3D (Cinema 4D) appears only as an optional plus in a brand-design posting, not in explainer postings. Blender, DaVinci, QGIS, Datawrapper and Flourish are not mentioned in any Vox source I found.

### Cited Findings
- After Effects, Photoshop, Illustrator are the three tools listed for the Art & Motion Graphics Director role on the Vox video team; the posting also lists typography, motion and data visualisation skill, plus experience editing and sourcing archival footage. Listing removed Oct 29 2025. — [Built In listing](https://builtin.com/job/art-motion-graphics-director/6985404)
- Same posting: the director owns and iterates the Vox video style guides as tools, templates and rules, helps producers develop their own look within Vox, manages freelancers, and mentors producers on design and animation. — [Built In listing](https://builtin.com/job/art-motion-graphics-director/6985404)
- Animator / Motion Designer for the Unexplainable video podcast: After Effects, Photoshop, Illustrator; a "playful visual look" while keeping Vox style and standards; delivers a graphics package plus per-episode graphics. Undated. — [Remotivated listing](https://remotivated.com/jobs/animator-motion-designer-unexplainable-part-time-at-vox-media)
- Motion Design Lead on the branded content team (Vox Explainer Studio, New York Magazine brands): After Effects, Photoshop, Illustrator, Figma; motion, VFX and 3D are core skills but no 3D software is named; sets motion standards and brand guidelines. Posted around early 2026 (unconfirmed). — [Built In NYC](https://www.builtinnyc.com/job/motion-design-lead/11444852)
- Senior Motion Designer on Vox Media's Brand Design team: advanced After Effects, Adobe Creative Suite incl. InDesign, Cinema 4D or equivalent a plus; style frames and storyboards; music and sound added to motion packages. Undated; brand-side, not explainer. — [ShowbizJobs](https://www.showbizjobs.com/jobs/vox-media-senior-motion-designer-in-new-york/jid-g673j8)
- Google Earth Studio: Sam Ellis workflow is find the location, keyframe a camera path, export a JPEG sequence, import to After Effects, link to a master world map, zoom country to site, then draw lines, arrows and labels. Orbit and spiral camera functions; keyframe system feels like AE so it was easy to adopt. Also used on Vox Video science/tech/history stories. — [Google Earth blog on Medium, Jul 12 2019](https://medium.com/google-earth/how-vox-video-uses-earth-studio-for-dynamic-visual-storytelling-703fc871766e)
- Johnny Harris (Vox Borders creator, left Vox; this is his own channel workflow, Dec 11 2020): After Effects plus the paid GEOlayers plugin; custom base map in Mapbox Studio (start from a preset, hide clutter); country outlines from Natural Earth via GEOlayers; older workflow used a layered Illustrator world map, nulls and masked solids. — [Johnny Harris "How I make my maps" transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/johnnyharris/how-i-make-my-maps)
- aescripts case-study page for a Johnny Harris volcano map: GEOlayers 3 globe template, Deep Glow, Video Copilot Orb, film grain as a last texture step. Undated. — [aescripts](https://aescripts.com/learn/making-maps-for-johnny-harris---volcanoes)
- For the Kamala Harris 7 moments video, corner pinning in After Effects to put clips on monitor screens (a 2.5D effect, not real 3D). — [Storybench, Nov 4 2024](https://www.storybench.org/?p=22821)
- Phil Edwards (ex-Vox, post-Vox practice) used 3D software for object shots and Unreal Engine for a game-show video. — [The Explainers newsletter, Feb 2024](https://theexplainers.substack.com/p/making-the-trivialnontrivial-how)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: Mapbox/Natural Earth/GEOlayers appear to be Johnny Harris's personal workflow; Sam Ellis's Atlas workflow is documented as Earth Studio plus AE plus hand-traced maps. Do not assume they were one Vox standard.

### Gaps
- Blender, DaVinci Resolve, QGIS (Ellis only says "some basic GIS"), Datawrapper, Flourish: no Vox video source found.
- Which AE plugins Vox staff use beyond what is above (Deep Glow, Orb, GEOlayers) is not documented by staff.
